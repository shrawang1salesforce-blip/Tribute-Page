01 / The story

Curiosity was her compass.

Marie Curie was a Polish-born physicist and chemist who became a defining scientific voice of the twentieth century.

Born Maria Skłodowska in Warsaw, she moved to Paris to pursue an education that had been closed to her at home. There, she met Pierre Curie, her scientific partner and husband.

Together they investigated the mysterious energy released by uranium. Their work led to the discovery of two elements—polonium and radium—and gave a name to the phenomenon: radioactivity.

02 / The milestones

A bright trail
through the dark.

Her discoveries made the invisible measurable—and opened new paths for medicine, physics, and generations of women in science.

  1. 1891

    A new city

    Maria arrives in Paris and begins studying physics and mathematics at the Sorbonne.

  2. 1898

    New elements

    Marie and Pierre Curie announce the discovery of polonium and radium.

  3. 1903

    First Nobel Prize

    She shares the Nobel Prize in Physics for pioneering research into radioactivity.

  4. 1911

    A second Nobel

    She wins the Nobel Prize in Chemistry, becoming the first person to win twice.

  5. 1914

    Science in service

    She develops mobile X-ray units to help doctors treat wounded soldiers during World War I.

03 / The legacy

The work continues.

Curie’s name lives on in the curie unit, the element curium, and in every researcher who chooses to ask one more question.
